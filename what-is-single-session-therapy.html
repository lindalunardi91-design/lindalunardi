What is Single Session Therapy?

By Linda Lunardi · Counsellor & Psychotherapist

Single Session Therapy, often called SST, is an approach to counselling that treats each session as complete and meaningful in itself. Rather than assuming therapy will unfold over many weeks or months, SST begins with a simple question: what would be most useful to you today?

Where does it come from?

SST was developed by Professor Windy Dryden, one of Britain’s leading psychotherapists. It draws on research showing that many people who seek therapy attend only one session — and that many of them find it genuinely helpful. SST takes this seriously, rather than treating a single session as an incomplete version of something longer.

How is it different from regular therapy?

In ongoing therapy, the first session is often about getting to know each other before the real work begins. In SST, we move more purposefully. We identify what you most want to address and what would be most useful to take away. The session has a clear focus — without feeling rushed.

This doesn’t mean SST is therapy at speed. It is a different way of working entirely, with its own philosophy: that people have more inner resources than they realise, and that even one conversation, held well, can shift something meaningful.

Who is it suited to?

SST suits people who have a specific concern to explore, those who are curious about therapy but not yet ready for an ongoing commitment, or those whose circumstances make regular sessions difficult right now. Research consistently shows that many people find a single session enough — leaving with a new perspective, greater clarity, or simply the feeling of having been heard.

What happens after?

There is no expectation to continue. If further work feels helpful, that option is always open. But the session is designed to stand on its own — complete, not preliminary.
